# Partnership Dissolution Agreement

## Description

This prompt generates a Partnership Dissolution Agreement that formally outlines how a business partnership will end. It covers division of assets and liabilities, handling of intellectual property, termination of obligations, and dispute resolution. providing a clean, legal closure for all parties involved.

## Best Use Cases

- Creating a structured first draft before professional review.
- Gathering the business details needed for a policy, agreement, or compliance document.
- Helping a founder or operator understand what questions need to be answered before finalizing the document.

## Workflow Prompt

```text
Your task is to help me draft a Partnership Dissolution Agreement. Please ask the following questions one at a time. Once complete, generate a full agreement that clearly outlines how the partnership will be dissolved.
1. What is the legal name of the business partnership?
2. What are the names and addresses of the partners involved?
3. Why is the partnership ending? (e.g., mutual agreement, partner exit, dispute)
4. What is the effective date of dissolution?
5. How will business assets be divided? (cash, property, equipment, IP, etc.)
6. How will liabilities and debts be handled?
7. Are there any jointly owned clients, contracts, or accounts that need transfer or closure?
8. Will one partner continue operating the business, or will it be fully shut down?
9. How will intellectual property (logos, brand names, content, etc.) be divided or licensed?
10. What happens to outstanding invoices, taxes, or legal obligations?
11. Are there any non-disparagement, confidentiality, or non-compete clauses to include?
12. How will disputes be resolved after the agreement is signed?
13. In what jurisdiction should the agreement be governed?
Once all questions are answered, write a full Partnership Dissolution Agreement including:
- Statement of mutual agreement to dissolve
- Effective date and definitions
- Allocation of assets and liabilities
- Handling of contracts, vendors, and clients
- Terms for continuing or closing the business
- Distribution of IP, websites, trademarks, and other rights
- Confidentiality, non-disparagement, or other protective clauses (optional)
- Final release of claims and liabilities
- Dispute resolution process and legal venue
- Signatures and witness section (if applicable) Use clear and formal language to reflect a professional, legally binding separation. 3—
```

## Usage Tips

- Use this when ending any multi-partner business to prevent future misunderstandings or legal disputes.
- Clearly define how assets, clients, and liabilities will be handled to ensure both parties walk away with written terms.
- Have both parties sign and store a digital and physical copy.
- You may want a legal review if large assets, trademarks, or clients are involved.
- Consider combining this with a final profit/loss report or statement of accounts.

## Review Notes

Have the final document reviewed by a qualified professional before using it. Confirm that it matches your jurisdiction, business model, customer relationships, and any industry-specific requirements.
