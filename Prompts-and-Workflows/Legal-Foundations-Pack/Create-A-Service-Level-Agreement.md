# Create A Service Level Agreement

## Description

This prompt helps businesses generate a Service Level Agreement (SLA) that clearly defines the service provider's performance expectations, response times, uptime guarantees, and remedies if those expectations aren't met.

## Best Use Cases

- Creating a structured first draft before professional review.
- Gathering the business details needed for a policy, agreement, or compliance document.
- Helping a founder or operator understand what questions need to be answered before finalizing the document.

## Workflow Prompt

```text
Your goal is to create a clear, enforceable SLA tailored to the business's service offering and customer expectations.

User Questions (Gather Information):

Ask the user:
1. What is the business name and official contact information?
2. What specific service does the SLA cover? (e.g., software hosting, IT support, managed services)
3. Who is the SLA intended for? (individual customers, business clients, enterprise partners)
4. What are the service availability guarantees? (e.g., 99.9% uptime)
5. What is the response time for support requests? (e.g., initial response within 2 hours)
6. What is the resolution time for issues? (e.g., critical issues resolved within 24 hours)
7. How can customers report issues or request support? (email, ticketing system, phone)
What compensation or remedies are offered if the SLA is breached? (service credits, refunds, termination
8. rights)
9. What exclusions apply (force majeure, scheduled maintenance, third-party failures)?
10. In which jurisdiction should the SLA be legally governed?

SLA Drafting Instructions:

Draft a complete Service Level Agreement including:
Introduction & purpose
Scope of services covered
Performance metrics (uptime, response times, resolution times)
Reporting and escalation process
Remedies for service failures (credits, refunds, discounts)
Exclusions and limitations
Legal disclaimers and governing law
Signature and date fields (if signed agreement required)
```

## Usage Tips

- Customize SLAs for different service tiers (basic, premium, enterprise).
- Make sure guarantees are realistic and achievable based on your actual capabilities.
- If working with enterprise clients, expect negotiation. SLAs often go back and forth before signing.
- Use plain language to ensure non-technical customers understand what's covered.
- Review annually to keep aligned with evolving service offerings.

## Review Notes

Have the final document reviewed by a qualified professional before using it. Confirm that it matches your jurisdiction, business model, customer relationships, and any industry-specific requirements.
